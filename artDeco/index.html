Exceptional English Art Deco Burr Walnut Extending Table

Dining / boardroom table · circa 1930–1935 · possibly London
A substantial and unusually sculptural English Art Deco extending table, believed to date from circa 1930–1935 and possibly made in London.

Description

Constructed principally in highly figured burr walnut veneer with contrasting walnut elements, the table has a distinctive twin-pedestal design. Each pedestal consists of a tall rectangular structure surrounded by closely spaced, rounded horizontal ribs, creating a striking stacked or corrugated architectural effect. The pedestals terminate in broad, stepped and shaped feet, also richly veneered in figured burr walnut.

The tabletop measures 200 × 100 cm when closed and extends to 300 × 100 cm with two approximately 50 cm extensions. It comprises four sections, discreetly marked I–IV on the underside, apparently as cabinetmaker's assembly or positioning marks.

The central surface accommodates a removable dark smoked/black glass protective panel. The glass is not structurally integral to the table and can be removed. The wooden surface beneath is comparatively plain, apart from the veneered edges visible around the glass.

The underside retains substantial traditional timber construction and wooden components associated with the extension mechanism. No maker's label, stamp or signature has been found. The table is stylistically characteristic of high-quality British/English Art Deco or Moderne furniture of the early 1930s. Its design and construction are comparable to furniture produced by leading London cabinetmakers, including Harry & Lou Epstein, although an Epstein attribution is not confirmed.

The table was purchased in Santiago, Chile, around 2000, without provenance or earlier documentation. It is now located in the Liège region of Belgium. The castors visible in the photographs are modern transport dollies and are not part of the table.
